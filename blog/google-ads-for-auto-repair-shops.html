Field guide · Auto service

Google Ads for auto repair shops: match the search to the service.

Drivers may need urgent diagnostics, routine maintenance, tires, or a repair estimate. A useful campaign connects each search to the service the shop can actually provide and the appointment path it can handle.

Separate urgent and planned service searches

Start with the services the shop wants and has capacity to perform. Urgent repair, maintenance, tires, and specialty work can carry different search intent and customer questions. Group them where the offer and destination page match; split them when one message cannot serve both intents clearly.

Build a page that helps someone call or book

A focused page should confirm the service, vehicles accepted, shop location, hours, booking method, and relevant policies. Show accurate pricing or offers when available, and explain what requires an inspection. Keep phone and appointment actions easy to use on a phone.

Control geography, schedules, and spend

Set locations based on the shop’s real draw area and monitor whether enquiries are serviceable. Use schedules that reflect actual call-answering hours or a reliable after-hours process. Set an affordable initial budget and review spend against qualified calls and appointments.

Measure the path beyond the click

Record whether calls were answered, whether the customer needed a service the shop offers, whether an appointment was booked, and what happened next. Click volume and call duration can help diagnose a campaign but do not prove revenue. Align tracking with privacy rules and customer expectations.

Start with the repair work your shop wants

A shop advertising diagnostics, brakes, tires, and major repairs may be competing for several different customer needs. Decide which services have available capacity and fit the business before building campaigns. Include vehicle or service restrictions on the relevant page so a driver can quickly understand whether your team is the right place to call.

A driver searching for a warning-light diagnosis should reach information about that process, not a generic promotion unrelated to the problem. Explain how to request an appointment, what information the advisor needs, and whether an online enquiry confirms a booking. Avoid quoting a final repair cost before the shop has assessed the vehicle.

Connect advertising with the service advisor

Ask the person answering calls which enquiries turn into useful appointments and which cannot be served. Record reasons such as unsupported vehicles, work outside the shop's scope, unavailable booking times, or customers seeking parts only. Those patterns can guide campaign exclusions and page updates more effectively than a click report on its own.

Make sure business hours and contact options match the actual front desk. If calls arrive after hours, provide an accurate callback expectation or a booking route the team can support. An automated reply can acknowledge an enquiry, but it should not promise a repair time or diagnosis that a technician has not confirmed.

Use completed repair orders to evaluate results

Track new enquiries through booked appointments, attended visits, and completed work. Separate existing customers from new customer acquisition when reviewing paid search. Repeat service can be valuable, but expected future visits should not be counted as revenue already earned. Keep the initial repair order and later repeat work visible as different measures.

Compare the value and direct costs of the work won, rather than choosing campaigns solely by the cheapest lead. A low-cost enquiry that never becomes an appointment does not fill a bay. Conversely, a more expensive enquiry can still make sense when the completed work supports the cost. The right comparison uses your shop's records and service mix.

Questions readers ask

Should a shop advertise every repair service?

Prioritize services with clear demand, margins, technician capability, available appointment capacity, and useful website information. Broaden only when the shop can serve the extra calls.

What is a good cost per lead for an auto repair shop?

It depends on service value, close rate, local competition, spend, and lead quality. Calculate your own allowable acquisition cost from completed jobs and contribution margin rather than copying an industry average.

Should I send every ad to the homepage?

Usually a focused service page makes it easier to match the ad and answer questions. The homepage may still fit broad brand searches or a shop with a single clear offer.
