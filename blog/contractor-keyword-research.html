Contractor SEO · Search intent

Contractor keyword research that starts with the jobs you want.

A useful keyword plan connects what a customer needs, what your company actually provides, and the page that can answer the search. It is not a list of phrases to repeat across every page.

Updated October 2026 · Arveno Growth editorial guide

For a contractor, good keyword research starts with the service and customer—not with a giant spreadsheet. Someone searching for an emergency furnace repair needs a different answer from someone planning a full HVAC replacement. Your site should make those distinctions easy to understand.

1. Build a service inventory before collecting search terms

List the jobs you perform, the customers you serve, which jobs are most valuable, where you can travel, and which work you have capacity to take on. Include common variants and exclusions. For example, “roof repair,” “roof replacement,” and “storm inspection” can have different urgency, scope, and qualification needs.

2. Group searches by intent

Useful groups often include service research, urgent repair, replacement or installation, pricing, comparison, and hiring intent. Questions such as “how much does a deck cost?” may need an educational guide, while “deck builder near me” belongs on a service page that explains location, project fit, process, and how to request an estimate.

  • Service intent: “roof repair contractor,” “garage door spring repair,” “auto AC repair.”
  • Commercial investigation: “best HVAC company,” “cost to replace a roof,” “Google Ads agency for contractors.”
  • Informational: “how long does a deck build take?” or “what does an HVAC maintenance visit include?”
  • Local intent: “tree removal near me” or a service plus a genuine town or service area.

3. Map each intent to one best page

Choose the most useful destination for each search group: a service page, industry page, local page with real local details, or an educational article. Avoid producing many near-identical pages that only swap a city name. Each page should answer a distinct need and have a clear role in your site.

For example, connect a dedicated roofing marketing page to a Google Ads service page, an article about Google Ads vs. SEO, and your paid-search calculator where that helps readers make a decision.

4. Review demand and competition carefully

Use Search Console queries, call notes, estimate requests, your advertising search-term reports, and keyword tools as clues. Volume estimates vary by tool and location; they do not predict leads or revenue. Prioritize relevance, service profitability, competition, seasonality, and the team’s ability to respond.

5. Write pages for people first, then make them easy to understand

Use a clear page title and heading, concise service descriptions, original project information, useful FAQs, accessible links, and descriptive images. Explain what is included, where you work, how the process goes, and what a customer can do next. Keep titles and descriptions distinct when the page itself has a distinct purpose.

6. Create a useful internal-link path

Link from broad service or industry pages to the more specific pages that answer the next question. Link educational guides back to relevant services and conversion tools. Use descriptive link text so visitors and search systems can understand the destination. Check for broken URLs and fragments whenever a page moves.

7. Measure qualified outcomes, not just rankings

Review relevant impressions and clicks alongside calls, forms, qualified estimates, booked work, and revenue when reliable records exist. Separate organic and paid traffic. A ranking change, AI citation, or high click-through rate is not by itself proof of business impact.

For national and local targeting: a company can work remotely across the United States and still describe its real service territories. Location pages should offer accurate, useful information about an actual area served. Do not imply a physical office or create repetitive city pages just to capture searches.

Contractor keyword research · FAQs

Should every keyword have its own page?

No. Group close variants when they share the same customer need. Build a dedicated page when the service or intent genuinely requires a different answer.

Should I create a page for every city in the United States?

No. Create useful location content for the territory your company genuinely serves. Near-identical city pages can frustrate users and violate search spam policies.

Can keyword research guarantee first-page rankings?

No. It improves planning and relevance, but search visibility depends on competition, site quality, the business, and search engine systems. No ranking is guaranteed.

How do AI search results use website content?

AI-enabled search may draw on accessible, useful web content, but inclusion and citations are not guaranteed. Clear structure and accurate information help people and systems interpret a page.
