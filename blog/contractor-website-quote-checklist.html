Website checklist

Before rebuilding your contractor website, check the path to a quote.

Use this practical review to find friction and missing information before investing in a redesign or more traffic.

A new website should make it easier for the right customer to understand your work and ask a useful question. Before commissioning a full rebuild, review the current path from the first page view to your team’s follow-up.

Make the offer specific

  • Are your main services named in language customers understand?
  • Do service pages explain what is included, what affects the scope, and how the process works?
  • Are the locations you can serve stated accurately?
  • Can visitors tell which jobs are a good fit?

Make proof verifiable

Use project photos with permission and context. Show real reviews accurately, preserve the reviewer’s meaning, and link to a source when appropriate. If a result is a client-reported outcome or a one-off example, label it that way. Never use a mockup as though it were a client project.

Check the next step on a phone

Call the business from the header and footer. Complete the quote form yourself. Keep only questions that help the team prepare or qualify the enquiry. If the form has multiple steps, show progress, maintain keyboard accessibility, and make the next step clear.

Check speed, trust, and accessibility basics

Compress large images, avoid unnecessary scripts, use readable contrast, label fields, provide useful error messages, and verify keyboard navigation. Keep important service details as readable page text instead of hiding them in images or animation.

Connect the site to follow-up

Confirm where form submissions go, who sees missed-call notifications, and how consent and privacy notices are handled. Record campaign source where appropriate, then compare enquiries with qualified leads and booked estimates. A form count alone can hide spam or poor-fit requests.

Decide whether to improve or rebuild

If the current site has a sound structure, improving a few service pages and the quote path may be enough. A rebuild may make sense when the site is difficult to update, inaccessible, insecure, or unable to support the sales process. Ask for a clear scope and ownership of the domain, hosting, and finished files.

Walk through the page as a first-time customer

Open the service page on a phone and assume you have never heard of the business. Can you tell what work it accepts, where it operates, and how to ask for an estimate without opening several menus? The first screen should establish the service and the next step. Detailed project information can follow, but essential contact information should be easy to reach.

Read the headings without the paragraphs. They should describe the questions a customer needs answered, such as project scope, the estimate process, and what happens after contact. Replace vague section names with concrete information. A page that is easy to scan helps someone find the detail relevant to their particular project.

Test the form beyond the submit button

Complete every required field using a realistic test enquiry, with the receiving team warned in advance. Check the error messages, the confirmation screen, the notification destination, and the record created in your lead system. A form that appears to submit successfully can still lose the enquiry if the notification fails or reaches an inbox nobody monitors.

Repeat the check with the on-screen phone keyboard open. Labels should remain visible, inputs should be large enough to use, and errors should explain how to fix the problem. Keep optional project notes separate from the few details required for a callback. If a website link or photograph would help, explain how to provide it without making it a barrier for everyone.

Check proof, claims, and the page's next step

Project photographs should load clearly, have permission for use, and represent the work being discussed. Testimonials should preserve the customer's meaning and identify their source where possible. Remove badges, performance figures, or availability claims the business cannot support. Specific, accurate information gives the customer a stronger basis for a decision.

Finish with a clear estimate invitation that fits the service. Avoid presenting a submitted enquiry as a confirmed booking unless the system really reserves a slot. Make sure phone links work and that navigation does not cover the page on a small screen. Contractor website planning · FAQs

Should I put a price list on the website?

Publish prices or ranges when they are accurate and useful; explain what changes the final scope.

What proof belongs on a service page?

Use permitted project photos, verifiable reviews, qualifications, and clear process details relevant to the service.

How many fields should a quote form have?

Ask only what helps route or prepare for the conversation. Reduce friction and explain how information is used.
