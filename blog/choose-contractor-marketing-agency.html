Agency checklist

Choose a marketing partner by how clearly they connect work to outcomes.

A practical set of questions for comparing an agency, freelancer, or in-house plan without getting distracted by rankings or big promises.

When contractors compare marketing companies, polished decks and promised lead counts can make the choice harder. Start by checking whether the team understands your services, service areas, margins, sales process, and operational capacity.

Ask what they will actually do

Ask for the first 30–60 days of work in plain language. The answer should say what will be reviewed, what will be changed, what access is needed, how leads will be measured, and who is responsible. “Full-service marketing” is not a scope by itself.

Ask how you keep access and ownership

  • Are ad accounts, analytics, website, domain, and call tracking set up under your business?
  • Can you retain access if you stop working together?
  • Who owns copy, design files, landing pages, and campaign history?
  • How are passwords and permissions managed?

Good operational habits reduce avoidable lock-in and make a handoff possible.

Ask how reporting follows the sales process

Request a sample report with spend, calls, forms, qualified leads, estimates, and won work where the data is available. Clarify what is directly measured and what relies on manual updates. A click or form submission is not automatically a good lead, and a booked job may have several contributing touchpoints.

Compare fees and media budgets separately

Ask what the agency fee covers, which platform costs are paid directly by you, what requires a separate project fee, and what happens when scope changes. If a performance guarantee is offered, ask for its written definitions, time period, exclusions, attribution method, and remedy. Read the agreement before treating a guarantee as part of your expected return.

Check proof carefully

Ask whether examples are real, whether the client approved public use, what period they cover, and whether the displayed outcome can be tied to the agency’s work. Look for context: service, geography, starting point, spend, and measurement. Avoid making a decision on a ranking screenshot or revenue figure without knowing how it was verified.

Notice how they handle uncertainty

Search demand changes by market and season. A credible partner explains what is known, what must be tested, and what would cause the plan to change. Be cautious of guaranteed first-place rankings or a fixed volume of leads before anyone has reviewed your business and market.

Use this checklist in a conversation with any provider, including our team. Compare the answers with the actual contract and scope. See our contractor marketing services or send a growth brief.

Ask an agency to diagnose a real problem

Bring one specific concern to the first conversation: unqualified calls, a weak estimate pipeline, an unclear website, or poor visibility for a service you want to grow. Ask how the agency would investigate it before recommending a package. A useful answer identifies the information needed, the possible causes, and the first decision the review should support.

Be cautious when every concern leads immediately to more advertising spend. More traffic cannot repair a broken form or make an unanswered phone useful. The proposal should connect the recommended work to the problem you described, with a clear explanation of what the agency will deliver and what your team needs to provide.

Make two proposals comparable

Put the monthly service fee, advertising budget, setup charges, and any separate software costs side by side. Then compare the actual work: campaign management, landing pages, reporting, content, call tracking, and follow-up support. A lower headline price may cover fewer responsibilities, while a longer list of deliverables may include work your business does not need.

Ask who owns the advertising account, domain, website files, and reporting data. Confirm how access works during the relationship and what is handed over if it ends. Review cancellation terms and any performance guarantee in writing. A guarantee should define the measurement period, the conditions, and the remedy rather than relying on an impressive headline alone.

Decide how you will judge progress together

Agree on a small set of outcomes your team can update reliably. For a contractor, that might include qualified enquiries, estimates booked, proposals sent, and confirmed jobs. Ask to see how the report distinguishes those stages. Platform conversions should not automatically be described as customers, and revenue should not be described as profit.

Choose a review rhythm that fits the sales cycle and assign someone on your team to share outcome information. An agency cannot learn which enquiries became good work if that information stays outside the reporting process. Choosing an agency · FAQs

Who owns the ad account and website?

Confirm ownership and admin access to accounts, domains, analytics, creative, and records in writing before work starts.

What should a useful report contain?

Look for spend, qualified enquiries, follow-up, outcomes, time period, and limitations—not just impressions or clicks.

How can I check a case study?

Ask for the client’s permission, source, currency, measurement window, and attribution method; distinguish client-reported figures from verified ones.
