Agency checklist

Choose a marketing partner by how clearly they connect work to outcomes.

A practical set of questions for comparing an agency, freelancer, or in-house plan without getting distracted by rankings or big promises.

When contractors compare marketing companies, polished decks and promised lead counts can make the choice harder. Start by checking whether the team understands your services, service areas, margins, sales process, and operational capacity.

Ask what they will actually do

Ask for the first 30–60 days of work in plain language. The answer should say what will be reviewed, what will be changed, what access is needed, how leads will be measured, and who is responsible. “Full-service marketing” is not a scope by itself.

Ask how you keep access and ownership

  • Are ad accounts, analytics, website, domain, and call tracking set up under your business?
  • Can you retain access if you stop working together?
  • Who owns copy, design files, landing pages, and campaign history?
  • How are passwords and permissions managed?

Good operational habits reduce avoidable lock-in and make a handoff possible.

Ask how reporting follows the sales process

Request a sample report with spend, calls, forms, qualified leads, estimates, and won work where the data is available. Clarify what is directly measured and what relies on manual updates. A click or form submission is not automatically a good lead, and a booked job may have several contributing touchpoints.

Compare fees and media budgets separately

Ask what the agency fee covers, which platform costs are paid directly by you, what requires a separate project fee, and what happens when scope changes. If a performance guarantee is offered, ask for its written definitions, time period, exclusions, attribution method, and remedy. Read the agreement before treating a guarantee as part of your expected return.

Check proof carefully

Ask whether examples are real, whether the client approved public use, what period they cover, and whether the displayed outcome can be tied to the agency’s work. Look for context: service, geography, starting point, spend, and measurement. Avoid making a decision on a ranking screenshot or revenue figure without knowing how it was verified.

Notice how they handle uncertainty

Search demand changes by market and season. A credible partner explains what is known, what must be tested, and what would cause the plan to change. Be cautious of guaranteed first-place rankings or a fixed volume of leads before anyone has reviewed your business and market.
