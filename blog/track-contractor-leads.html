Measurement guide

Track the lead journey from first contact to booked work.

A practical measurement framework for contractors who want to understand which campaigns create useful conversations—not just clicks.

Lead tracking is the process of recording how a prospective customer found you and what happened next. It helps you see where follow-up breaks down and whether marketing is creating the conversations your business wants.

Agree on what counts as a lead

Write down which calls and forms count as a new enquiry. Decide how to handle existing customers, suppliers, job applicants, spam, missed calls, and duplicate requests. If your team uses a different definition for each channel, the report will be difficult to compare.

Measure the steps you can act on

  • Source or campaign, when it can be captured reliably.
  • First contact date and whether a human response happened.
  • Service and location fit.
  • Estimate scheduled, sent, accepted, declined, or still open.
  • Booked job and known revenue, with the date the information was confirmed.

Do not collect personal information you do not need. Choose tools and retention practices that fit your privacy obligations and explain tracking as required.

Test the whole path

Submit a real test enquiry from a phone. Confirm the form works, notifications reach the right person, the customer sees a confirmation, and the lead appears in the place your team uses. Test call measurement from a mobile device as well. Repeat after major site or tag changes.

Use attribution with care

A customer may see an ad, return later through organic search, call after a referral, and then book. No single report perfectly represents that journey. State whether a report uses first-touch, last-touch, a platform-reported conversion, or a manually confirmed source. Keep the label visible so a decision-maker knows what the number means.

A useful weekly review asks: Which leads fit our service and area? Which have not received a response? What happened to estimates? What do we know about the source, and what remains unknown?

Make follow-up part of the system

Assign an owner, define a reasonable response expectation for your team, and record the next action. Marketing cannot improve a lead that disappears into an inbox. If volume grows, use a CRM or shared process that staff will consistently maintain.

Build a record your team will actually maintain

Start with a small set of fields: enquiry date, customer identifier, service requested, source when known, assigned owner, current status, and next action. Add the estimate and job outcome when those events occur. A shared spreadsheet can be enough for a small team if access is appropriate and someone maintains it consistently; a CRM becomes more useful as handoffs and volume grow.

Write a short definition for each status. An estimate requested is different from an estimate scheduled, and a sent proposal is different from accepted work. Give staff a clear way to mark a duplicate, an existing customer, or an enquiry outside the service area. Those distinctions prevent inflated lead totals and make follow-up lists more useful.

Keep marketing events separate from business outcomes

A tracked form submission confirms that an event occurred on the website. It does not establish that the person is suitable, that an appointment took place, or that a job was sold. Preserve the original event while adding the later business outcome. This lets you see both campaign activity and the quality of the work it creates.

Use consistent dates when reviewing performance. A job sold this month may have started with an enquiry several months earlier. Grouping enquiries by their first contact period can help you understand how they develop over time. Keep open opportunities visible rather than treating every undecided customer as lost at the end of the calendar month.

Turn a weekly review into a short action list

Look first for unanswered enquiries and overdue next steps. Then review why unsuitable contacts arrived and whether the same issue appears repeatedly. Finally, compare confirmed outcomes across services and sources where the records are complete enough. Assign each improvement to a person: return a call, clarify a page, adjust targeting, or follow up on an estimate.

Make uncertainty explicit. If source information is missing, label it unknown rather than guessing. If revenue is booked but not collected, say so. Lead tracking questions · FAQs

Which phone calls should I count?

Define qualification before reporting, then separate wrong numbers, vendors, existing customers, and genuine new opportunities where possible.

How can I track a lead through an estimate?

Use a consistent status and source in your CRM or job system, then train the team to update it.

What if our records are incomplete?

Start with a small reliable set of statuses and improve completeness over time; avoid claiming precision your data cannot support.
