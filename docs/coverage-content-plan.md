# Coverage content and measurement plan

Updated 7 October 2026. Scope: `/coverage/` and the distinct `/coverage/ontario.html` guide.

## Implemented

The main coverage page groups all 50 states once, explains Canadian coverage and remote delivery, and relates territory planning to trade, budget, crew capacity, lead routing, and sales outcomes. Regions are navigation/discussion groups, not invented market data or claimed offices. Ontario has a substantive guide covering practical municipality groupings, real dispatch boundaries, CAD/USD clarity, time zones, local content, and phased market expansion. Both pages link to relevant services, industries, and the homepage audit form. Neither implies local offices or a customer history in every area.

The Ontario guide is distinct from the nationwide directory: it helps a business define an Ontario territory and avoid currency, geography, and scheduling assumptions. It is not a template for fifty near-duplicate pages. The main agent must include its new route in the Vite build; the existing sitemap generator will then include its canonical automatically.

## Evidence used

- [Google local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), checked 7 October 2026: local relevance, distance, and prominence; coverage text does not create proximity.
- [Google Ads location targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043?hl=en), checked 7 October 2026: location signals are imperfect; monitor outcomes and choose actual service boundaries.
- [Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), checked 7 October 2026: ordinary SEO foundations apply, with no guaranteed citation.
- [Ontario municipality directory](https://www.ontario.ca/page/list-ontario-municipalities) and [About Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/about-ontario), checked 7 October 2026, used for geographic context. No population forecast or unsupported lead-cost comparison was imported into the copy. Groupings on the site are practical discussion examples, explicitly not official advertising regions.

## Data needed before expanding location content

1. Search Console query/page/country data with a date range, separating brand and nonbrand demand. This identifies which existing pages already answer searches and avoids internal competition.
2. Keyword Planner or authorized campaign exports for selected services and actual target markets. Record currency, date, targeting, match interpretation, and available search-volume ranges. No search-volume figures have been invented.
3. Client service boundaries, branches, approved public location details, working hours, languages supported, and travel limitations by job type.
4. Enquiry-to-job records with source, location, qualified status, quoted/won/completed state, revenue, direct costs, and campaign spend. Distinguish net-new from existing customers and duplicates.
5. Local-search observations with query, precise search location, date, and device. One map screenshot is not nationwide coverage evidence.
6. A bounded AI-search observation set: exact commercial questions, market/locale, engine, date, repetitions, linked citations, and referral outcomes. Content accessibility alone does not prove visibility.

## Next-page decision

Create a location page only when actual service coverage and distinct buyer information justify it. Useful evidence could include approved local project records, location-specific services, branch procedures, real customer questions, or meaningful seasonal delivery arrangements. Otherwise improve the existing service, industry, or coverage page. Avoid bulk city-name substitution, invented local proof, fabricated links, or promised near-me/AI placement.

## Deployment verification

After the coordinating agent builds, verify both canonical routes, sitemap membership, internal anchors, one H1 per page, mobile card wrapping, keyboard access to FAQs, and the homepage audit link. Live crawling/indexing and Search Console are separate checks requiring the deployed domain and account access.
