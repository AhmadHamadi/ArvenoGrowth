Three different customer journeys

Repairs and urgent service

A person with a broken garage door, a plumbing problem, or a car that will not start needs to know whether the business handles the problem, serves the location, and can respond. A concise mobile page and a clear call path matter more than a long project gallery. Campaign targeting should reflect the work the team can actually accept, and the office needs a way to distinguish an enquiry from a booked visit.

Projects that need an estimate

Decks, renovations, landscaping, and replacement installations usually require more discussion. The customer wants to understand project fit, materials, timing, and what happens at an estimate. Useful marketing helps them describe the work and arrive at that conversation prepared. A form can collect the essential project details while leaving room for questions, rather than forcing a price before the scope is understood.

Ongoing and repeat services

Maintenance and repeat-service businesses need to consider scheduling, retention, and the value of the first visit separately. The first job may not describe the whole relationship, but future revenue should not be assumed before it is measured. Keep acquisition reporting clear, then evaluate repeat work through the business's own records.
