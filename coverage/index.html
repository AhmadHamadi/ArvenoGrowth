Working with Canadian businesses

We also support Canadian contractors and service businesses remotely. Our public work includes Ontario businesses, identified by their actual locations. For a Canadian engagement, we confirm the provinces and municipalities served, the account currency, customer language, working hours, and where enquiries should go. A campaign targeting a Canadian customer should use the business's real service details and appropriate local contact information.

For Ontario businesses, see our Ontario marketing and service-area guide. It explains how we plan around the GTA and Hamilton, Southwestern Ontario, Eastern Ontario, and Northern Ontario without treating an entire province as one local market.

If you operate in both countries, separate the budgets and reporting where that makes costs, currencies, and sales outcomes clearer. Language requirements, advertising restrictions, and the services you can lawfully deliver must be confirmed for the actual market. We do not assume that an existing US campaign can be copied unchanged into Canada.

How we choose cities and service areas

  1. Map the work you can fulfill. Identify your base, branches, ZIP or postal codes, travel limits, and any locations you exclude. Different services may need different boundaries.
  2. Choose the jobs worth acquiring. Review margin, typical project value, response capacity, and whether your team wants repairs, installations, recurring service, or larger projects.
  3. Check the path to an enquiry. A customer needs a clear service description, accurate coverage, useful proof, and a working call or form. Leads need an owner once they arrive.
  4. Launch a manageable territory. Give the campaign enough focus to evaluate qualified enquiries and booked work. Expand when staffing, sales follow-up, and results support the decision.

With a monthly advertising budget around $700–$1,100, we would first discuss one priority service and a territory your business can handle. Splitting that budget across many distant cities makes it harder to learn where the work is coming from. The appropriate scope depends on your market, actual account data, and job economics.

Match the campaign to the kind of business

For plumbing and electrical businesses, separate urgent calls from planned installations where their response requirements differ. For landscaping and deck builders, discuss project scope, site visits, and the future work schedule. For auto repair shops, consider appointment availability, accepted vehicles, and the services each location provides.

Our Google Ads management, local SEO, and lead tracking work connects those decisions. The same keyword does not necessarily represent the same customer need in every trade.

Useful local pages, accurate search information

A city page should help someone make a decision. Relevant information can include the services available there, genuine project examples, branch contact details, appointment arrangements, and travel restrictions. We recommend a separate page when those details make it useful. A list of city names alone does not explain why a customer should choose the business.

For “near me” searches, Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence. Accurate business details and genuine customer feedback support a trustworthy presence, but remote coverage does not create a local office. Read Google's local ranking guidance.

Paid geographic targeting is a separate tool. We review location settings and the enquiries received rather than assuming every click is inside the intended territory. Google explains that location targeting uses multiple signals and is not perfectly accurate in its location targeting guidance.

Working with a remote growth partner · FAQs

Can you work with my business in any US state?

Yes, our remote coverage includes all 50 states. We first confirm your service, territory, account access, and scope so the engagement fits your business.

Do you support businesses in Canada?

Yes. We confirm the province, local service boundaries, currency, language needs, and communication arrangements. Our published client examples include Canadian businesses and are identified that way.

How do meetings and response times work across time zones?

We agree on meeting times and communication expectations during onboarding. Campaign schedules and lead notifications should match the client's local operating hours. Remote support does not imply a staffed agency office or round-the-clock response in every time zone.

Can one plan cover several locations?

It can, if the scope and budget support it. Each branch needs clear service coverage, lead ownership, and reporting. We consider separate campaigns or pages when locations have materially different services, budgets, or customer information.

Will you create a page for every city?

We recommend city pages when a business actually serves the location and can offer distinct information that helps customers. Otherwise, a useful service-area page and focused service pages can be a better starting point.

Can a remote agency improve our local search presence?

We can help with accurate profile information, website content, technical issues, and measurement. Your actual business location and service details remain the foundation. Search engines decide rankings and AI citations.

What should I share before the first review?

Your website, services, city or service area, current advertising budget, and the kind of work you want more of are helpful. If available, bring recent enquiry and booked-job records. The free audit form is a straightforward place to start.

Do you have customers in every state?

No. Service availability describes where we can work remotely. It is separate from our client history, and we do not present Canadian projects as US case studies.
